# Business brief: Competitor analysis: Jumia vs Kilimall e-commerce Kenya

*19 November 2026 | 20 sources | 4 web searches | cost $0.0497*

Jumia Kenya reported record sales of Sh6.4 billion ($49.7 million) in the three months to June 2026, up 84.1% year-on-year, with fashion and beauty demand offsetting a slump in smartphones [1][3][20]. The momentum did not carry through the year. Jumia's Q3 2026 Kenya physical-goods GMV grew only 12% year-on-year as phones and electronics declined on supply constraints, and the company cut its full-year 2026 GMV growth outlook to 16-18%, sending its shares down 23% [4][5][6]. Jumia's advantage is reach beyond Nairobi: 67.6% of its Kenyan active customers are outside Nairobi, and upcountry orders made up 61% of total orders in Q2 2026 [2][7]. Kilimall's public data is thin and largely promotional. Its claims of 10 million products and 82% next-day delivery are unverified, and its only traffic figure, about 670 thousand visits in December 2022, is roughly four years old, so no reliable 2026 comparison with Jumia is possible [9][10][11][12]. Both platforms also face counterfeit-goods risk, with each reported as a leading source of counterfeits in Kenya [8][19].

For a Kenyan SME, Jumia's upcountry customer base is the clearest signal of where demand is growing, but its slowing electronics category and tightening growth outlook suggest it should not be treated as a guaranteed sales channel without testing. Kilimall should be evaluated on verified performance before any commitment, since its claims cannot yet be benchmarked. The reported Jumia commitment to accept direct legal liability for untraceable non-compliant vendors comes from a single secondary report and should be confirmed with the platform before it informs any listing decision. Next steps: run a limited pilot on Jumia focused on fashion and beauty categories where demand is strongest, request Kilimall's current traffic and delivery data directly, and make sure your own product sourcing and brand documentation are clean so your listings are not confused with counterfeit goods.

## Key points

- Jumia Kenya sales hit a record Sh6.4 billion ($49.7 million) in the three months to June 2026, up 84.1% year-on-year, driven by fashion and beauty demand offsetting a smartphone slump. [1][3][20]
- Jumia's Q3 2026 Kenya physical-goods GMV grew only 12% year-on-year as phones and electronics declined on supply constraints, and Jumia cut its full-year 2026 GMV growth outlook to 16-18%, sending shares down 23%. [4][5][6]
- Jumia's reach beyond Nairobi is a key differentiator: 67.6% of its Kenyan active customers are outside Nairobi, and upcountry orders were 61% of total orders in Q2 2026. [2][7]
- Kilimall's public data is thin and largely promotional: its 10 million product and 82% next-day delivery claims are unverified, and the only traffic figure (about 670 thousand visits in December 2022) is roughly four years old, so no reliable 2026 comparison with Jumia is possible. [9][10][11][12]
- Both platforms face counterfeit-goods risk, with Jumia and Kilimall reported as leading sources of counterfeits in Kenya, and Jumia has announced it will accept direct legal liability when a non-compliant vendor cannot be tracked, a claim from a single secondary report. [8][19]
