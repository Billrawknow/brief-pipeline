# Business brief: M-Pesa payment trends among Nairobi SMEs

*19 November 2026 | 14 sources | 4 web searches | cost $0.0491*

M-Pesa has become the dominant payment rail for Kenyan commerce. Transaction volume reached roughly 46.4 billion in FY2026, up about 25% year on year, with transaction value around Ksh41.68 trillion [1][2][3]. Note that the regulator-linked report cites a slightly different figure of 46.61 billion, and the 46.4 billion number is the one more widely reported [5]. Merchant acceptance is expanding fastest: the number of M-Pesa merchant businesses rose from about 1.8 million to roughly 3.13 million in a single year, a growth of about 71% [3][4]. Pricing is also shifting. From 7 August 2026, Safaricom doubled the free Lipa na M-Pesa collection threshold from Sh200 to Sh500, charges 0.55% on collections above that, capped at Sh200, and introduced a Pay to Mobile tariff that lets traders move daily takings to personal accounts [6]. Regulators are pressing costs lower: the Central Bank of Kenya's draft National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2025–2028 targets cutting average cost per transaction from Ksh23 to Ksh10 by 2028 and capping person-to-person fees, against a backdrop of 95.31 million registered mobile money accounts by August 2026 and person-to-merchant payments at about 30% of volume [9][10].

For a Kenyan SME, the implications are practical. Accepting M-Pesa is now close to a baseline requirement, since customers and suppliers increasingly expect it; national data shows 53% of small firms receive customer payments through mobile money and 61% pay suppliers that way [13]. The revised collection tariff makes small-ticket sales cheaper, but businesses with larger daily collections should model the 0.55% charge against their margins and use the Pay to Mobile option to separate business cash from personal funds. Next steps: review your monthly M-Pesa collection volumes against the new Sh500 threshold, reconcile transactions daily, and track your effective fee cost as a share of revenue. Because no recent survey isolates Nairobi SMEs, treat any Nairobi-specific benchmarks as unverified and rely on your own transaction records to guide pricing and cash-flow decisions [13][14].

## Key points

- M-Pesa volume reached roughly 46.4 billion transactions in FY2026, up about 25% year on year, with FY2026 transaction value around Ksh41.68 trillion; the report's own 46.61bn figure conflicts with the more widely reported 46.4bn. [1][2][3][5]
- The number of M-Pesa merchant businesses rose from about 1.8 million to roughly 3.13 million in a single year, a growth of about 71%, making merchant acceptance a fast-growing channel for SMEs. [3][4]
- From 7 August 2026, Safaricom doubled the free Lipa na M-Pesa collection threshold from Sh200 to Sh500, with collections above that charged 0.55% capped at Sh200, and added a Pay to Mobile tariff that lets traders move daily takings to personal accounts. [6]
- Regulators are pushing costs down: the CBK's draft National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2025–2028 targets cutting average cost per transaction from Ksh23 to Ksh10 by 2028 and capping P2P fees, while registered mobile money accounts reached 95.31 million by August 2026 and person-to-merchant payments are about 30% of volume. [9][10]
- No recent survey covers Nairobi SMEs specifically; the closest figures are national FSD-Kenya 2023 data showing 53% of small firms receive customer payments via mobile money and 61% pay suppliers that way, and older Nairobi-based studies, so Nairobi-specific claims should be treated as unverified until better data is available. [13][14]
