# Business brief: Agricultural financing options for smallholder farmers in Rift Valley

*19 November 2026 | 17 sources | 3 web searches | cost $0.0355*

Smallholder farmers in the Rift Valley rely most heavily on Saccos (32%) and commercial banks (29%) for agricultural credit, while digital mobile loans reach 13% and government channels such as the Hustler Fund and AFC account for only 4% each [5]. Commercial bank lending rates averaged 14.4% in September 2026, down from 17.2% in November 2024, after the Central Bank of Kenya held its benchmark rate at 8.75%, and agricultural lending grew 23.5% to Sh190.2 billion in the year to April 2026 [1][2][3]. The state lender AFC offers seasonal, livestock, farm improvement and land loans at reportedly about 10% fixed interest, though sources conflict, with some citing 8% [6][9]. Since July 2026, AFC has also offered collateral-free lending and warehouse-receipt financing for farmers lacking title deeds [10]. Meanwhile, the pending Crops Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 would merge AFC and the Commodities Fund into the Kenya Agribusiness Development Corporation, and Ministry data shows a KSh 42.2 billion shortfall in agricultural credit demand [6][11].

For a Kenyan SME serving the sector, these conditions point to a widening opportunity: falling bank rates, rising agricultural lending, and a persistent credit gap that formal lenders have not closed. Businesses that supply inputs, equipment or services can build partnerships with Saccos, banks and AFC to channel financing to farmers, while asset-financing models such as tractor loans with flexible seasonal repayments and high loan-to-value ratios have shown traction in the North Rift [13]. Digital and securitised finance is also scaling, with a May 2026 deal mobilising KES 276 million for about 23,839 smallholders and a target of reaching over 130,000 farmers [14][15]. SMEs should monitor the Crops Laws (Amendment) Bill closely, as the restructuring of AFC could alter eligibility and lending terms, and should verify current AFC interest rates directly before committing to pricing. Next steps include mapping which Rift Valley counties and value chains have the greatest unmet demand, engaging local Saccos as distribution partners, and preparing collateral-light offerings that align with the new AFC products.

- The state lender AFC offers seasonal, livestock, farm improvement and land loans, reportedly at about 10% fixed interest (sources conflict, with some citing 8%), and since July 2026 it offers collateral-free lending and warehouse-receipt financing for farmers lacking title deeds. [6][9][10]
